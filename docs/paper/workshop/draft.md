## Abstract

Training-free iterative pruning of large language models accepts or rejects each step with a gate: a perplexity
threshold, or a test of benchmark accuracy on a reused pool of questions. Neither gives a statistical guarantee for
the model that comes out. We pre-registered a screen-then-confirm study of how much compression can be certified per
domain. Each run's accepted checkpoints are frozen in order of compression and tested once on a sealed confirmation
set of 13,100 items, with a per-category non-inferiority test (an intersection-union test with Clopper–Pearson bounds,
a 3-point limit and α = 0.05) inside fixed-sequence testing. The matrix is two base models (SmolLM3-3B and
Granite-3.3-2B), three seeds and three screens: a perplexity rollback, a capability gate, and a capability gate with a
KL-probe fallback. Certified compression is small, 1.2–3.1 % of parameters. It is identical across the three screens
in 4 of 6 model–seed pairs, because confirmation failed before the screens diverged; in the other two pairs the KL
fallback certified one more step. The perplexity rollback never rejected a step. It ran 16–17 % of parameters past
what could be certified, to losses of 18–32 points in its worst category, yet after confirmation it certified as much
as the capability gate in every pair, at the lowest evaluation cost. None of the three pre-registered hypotheses we
tested is supported, and one could not have been under our design. With 500 confirmation items per category, the
median certified compression was zero in every run.

## 1. Introduction

Iterative, training-free structured pruning with an accept/reject gate is a simple recipe. A controller proposes a
pruning step; the step is kept if a check passes; repeat. Kodathala & Vunnam (2026) use a large language model as the
controller and a perplexity rollback (τ = 0.15) as the check.

Such gates are statistical decisions on reused data:
* a perplexity gate never looks at task accuracy;
* a capability gate tests accuracy on a finite pool of questions that it reuses for every candidate along the path.

A step-by-step check does not certify where the path ends. Zhang (2026) makes the general point: "a terminal guarantee
needs fresh control at each reached comparison or a trajectory-wide continuation bound".

We ask three questions:
* How much compression can be **certified** per domain (MMLU category) after training-free pruning?
* At what evaluation cost?
* Does the choice of screen change the certified compression?

By **certify** we mean a statistical claim, not formal verification. A checkpoint is certified when a one-sided test
rejects "this category loses at least 3 points of accuracy against the dense model" in every category, at α = 0.05,
on items the search never saw. In the terms of clinical statistics this is a non-inferiority test for paired binary
outcomes (Nam, 1997; Tango, 1998).

We pre-registered the plan, the data splits and the analysis, and we report the result, including the nulls.

**Contributions.**
1. **A pre-registered confirmatory study:** two models × three seeds × three screens, with sealed per-model
   confirmation sets and every amendment dated and committed before the scoring it governs (§3).
2. **The certified compression and why the screens coincide.** Under the shared-trajectory design, the confirmed
   comparison between screens is one-sided by construction (§4.1), and in most model–seed pairs the screens certify
   the same checkpoint.
3. **Overshoot and cost of each screen.** Measured as secondary endpoints added after the pilot (§5.5). A perplexity
   rollback that never rejects, followed by confirmation, certifies as much as the capability gate at lower cost.
4. **A certification frontier (EXPLORATORY):** how certified compression grows with the confirmation sample size and
   the limit (§6).

**What is not new:** the multiple-testing machinery (Learn then Test; Pareto Testing), non-inferiority tests for
paired proportions, the flip and KL metrics, and the multistep caveat. See §8.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Screening

**The pruning loop.**
* **Step:** each step removes a fixed block of MLP channels, chosen by gradient top-1. The candidate with the lowest
  mean gradient z-score is taken, over 4-layer depth windows.
* **Step size:** 18.9 M parameters for SmolLM3-3B-Base (3,075,098,624 parameters) and 15.7 M for Granite-3.3-2B-Base
  (2,533,525,504) ⟨curves⟩, ⟨study⟩.
* **Caps:** 30 gated proposals and 100,000 paired screening questions per run ⟨plan⟩ §3.

**The three screens:**
* **(a) Perplexity rollback.** Accept a step if perplexity on the C4 web-text validation set (32 × 2,048 tokens;
  Raffel et al., 2020) is at most 15 % above its parent's, the rollback threshold of Kodathala & Vunnam (τ = 0.15)
  ⟨plan⟩ §3. The comparison is with the previous checkpoint, not the dense model, so the allowed rise compounds
  along a run. (The corpus C4 is unrelated to hypothesis C4 below.)
* **(b) Capability gate.** An exact finite-pool, threshold-aware sequential test per MMLU category at a 3-point limit,
  α = 0.05 per side. It runs on an 11,132-item gate pool: STEM 4,231, Humanities 3,574, Social Sciences 954,
  Other 2,373 ⟨reg1⟩, ⟨reg2⟩. It stops at its first rejection.
* **(c) Capability gate + KL probe.** As (b), but after a rejection it scores ranks 2–11 on a 400-item probe set and
  gates the candidate that the KL signal picks: the lowest worst-category KL, ties to the lower rank
  (`capprune.signals.pick_action`). It stops when the KL pick is rejected.

**Shared trajectory.** All three screens make the same gradient top-1 steps from the same dense model and seed until
a screen first rejects. Arm (a) accepted 30 of 30 proposals in every run ⟨study⟩ (`runs[].accepts`). In this study it is therefore, in
effect, the unscreened gradient trajectory, stopped by the 30-proposal cap.

### 2.2 Why the per-step gate cannot certify the result

The capability gate's decisions are valid one step at a time, on its pool. Two things break the link to the model
that comes out:
* **Pool reuse under search.** The same pool is reused by every decision along a run. In our runs each gate-pool item
  was used 17–48 times on average, and the 954-item Social Sciences pool was used in full by 34–39 % of decisions
  ⟨mech⟩ (EXPLORATORY). Accepting the candidates that happen to look good on a reused pool is a selection effect.
* **No composition.** A sequence of per-step accept decisions, each at its own error level, does not bound the loss
  of the final checkpoint against the dense model. That needs a test of the final model itself on fresh data.

This is an argument about what the gate can guarantee. Whether its pool estimate was in fact optimistic in our runs
is hypothesis C1, and the answer is mixed (§5.1).

Certifying each step on fresh data instead would need more items per category than the pools hold. On the development
model (Qwen3-1.7B; exploratory, not part of this study), one step at zero true loss and the median observed
disagreement rate needed about 1,200–1,500 items per category for a 50 % chance of certifying 3 points, under the
population re-gating rule. The Social Sciences pool here holds 954 (`~/cap-prune-runs/population-regate/report.md`,
"Required pool sizes": n50 at the median disagreement rate, true loss 0).

### 2.3 Confirmation

The protocol is amendment 1 of `docs/screen_confirm_protocol.md`; ⟨plan⟩ §4.

**Freeze.** After a batch of runs ends, each run's accepted checkpoints are frozen in ascending order of parameters
removed. The order is written and hashed before any confirmation item is read.

**Confirm.** Each checkpoint is materialized and scored with the dense model, 0-shot, in identical batches, on the
model's sealed confirmation set:
* **Set size:** 13,100 items (STEM 3,600, Humanities 3,000, Social Sciences 3,000, Other 3,500) ⟨study⟩;
* **Sources:** MMLU test (7,317 items) and validation (788) (Hendrycks et al., 2021) and MMLU-CF validation (4,995)
  (Zhao et al., 2025). The split is seeded, stratified by category and, within a category, proportional to each
  source's size ⟨plan⟩ §2.
* **Deduplication:** before the split, 135 duplicate questions and 6 items equal to few-shot exemplars were dropped
  from the 25,573 pooled items ⟨plan⟩ §2.
* **Separation:** the screening sets (gate pool, probe, hold-out) share no item with the confirmation set. A run
  refuses to start otherwise, and only the confirmation command can read the sealed rows ⟨plan⟩ §2.

**Per-category bound.** With k⁺ items lost (dense right, checkpoint wrong) and k⁻ items gained among n, the one-sided
95 % upper bound on the paired loss Δ = p⁺ − p⁻ is cp_upper(k⁺, n, α/2) − cp_lower(k⁻, n, α/2).

**Decision.** A checkpoint passes if every category's bound is at most 3 points: an intersection-union test of the
union null "some category loses ≥ 3 points". Fixed-sequence testing over the frozen order stops at the first failure.
The run's claim is the last passing checkpoint.

**Guarantee.** This controls the probability of a false claim at α = 0.05 per run (Angelopoulos et al., Learn then
Test), under two conditions:
* **The checkpoints and their order are fixed before any confirmation item is read.** The freeze enforces this.
* **Each category's confirmation items are an independent sample from the population the claim is about.** That
  population is the category's mixture of MMLU and MMLU-CF questions in the proportions above, answered 0-shot. The
  claim says nothing about other tasks, prompts or few-shot settings.

The bound is conservative: Clopper–Pearson (1934) on each count, joined by a union bound. The intersection-union
principle is Berger's (1982; Berger & Hsu, 1996).

**Reading the ranges.** Each claim holds for its own run at α = 0.05. The ranges quoted in this paper are ranges over
runs. The largest value, 3.10 %, is the best of 18 runs, not a level the procedure can be expected to certify.

**Power.** The joint power of the test at a true loss of 1 point in every category was 0.45–0.68 (SmolLM3) and

**Why the claims are small.** One step removes about 0.6 % of parameters. At the first checkpoint past each claim
(steps 3–5 of arm (a)), the weakest category's estimated loss was already +2.1 to +4.2 points ⟨bdreal⟩. In three of
the six pairs that estimate was itself above 3 points, so no bound and no larger confirmation set would have
certified the checkpoint.

### 4.1 Why the screens coincide: a structural property

Arm (a) accepted every proposal, and all arms follow gradient top-1 from the same start. So arm (b)'s frozen order is
a prefix of arm (a)'s, and also of arm (c)'s. We checked this against the frozen orders and journals: the mask hashes
match up to the end of (b)'s screen in all six pairs ⟨report⟩.

The fixed-sequence claim therefore satisfies claim(a) ≥ claim(b) ≤ claim(c) by construction:
* **Confirmation fails inside the shared prefix** (SmolLM3, all seeds; Granite seed 2): all three arms claim the same
  checkpoint.
* **The gate stops first** (Granite seeds 0 and 1):
  * arm (b) certifies its whole screen;
  * the step it rejected fails confirmation in arm (a), so (a) = (b);
  * arm (c)'s KL replacement passed and was confirmed.

The primary comparison, as designed, could only show (b) ≤ (a) and (c) ≥ (b). It showed (b) = (a) in all six pairs, and (c) > (b) in two.

In both pairs where the gate was binding, the step it rejected also failed confirmation in arm (a). Two cases are too
few to say that the gate's rejections are reliable.

### 5.2 C4: does perplexity gating hide domain loss?

**Criterion:** in both models, arm (a) confirms less than arm (b) in ≥ 2 of 3 seeds, **and** arm (a)'s final screened
checkpoint exceeds 3 points in some category in ≥ 2 of 3 seeds.

**Not supported.**
* **The second half holds in all six seeds.** The perplexity screen accepted every step, to 18.4 % (SmolLM3) and
  18.6 % (Granite) of parameters removed. There, the estimated losses are +9.9 to +20.7 points (SmolLM3) and +14.3 to
  +32.1 points (Granite), worst in Social Sciences in every seed ⟨study⟩ `models[].c4`.
* **The first half never holds,** and it cannot. By §4.1, arm (a) always confirms at least as much as arm (b) under
  this design.

**C4 was a design flaw.** The pre-registered criterion compared confirmed compression, but the shared-trajectory
design makes "(a) confirms less than (b)" impossible. The fixed sequence protects arm (a)'s claim: confirmation stops
it after 2–4 steps, 26–28 steps before its screen ended. What C4 was meant to show appears instead as overshoot (S1,
§5.5) and in the loss curves (Figure 1).

**Perplexity along arm (a)** (post hoc; ⟨journals⟩ and ⟨curves⟩, tabulated in `tables/perplexity_arm_a.md`).
* **No step came near the threshold.** The largest single-step rise was 3.0 %, and at most 1.7 % in five of the
  six runs, against the 15 % allowed.
* **The claims sit at small perplexity changes.** The claimed checkpoints are 0.9–4.7 % above the dense model's
  perplexity, and the first failing ones 1.4–7.2 %.
* **The endpoints** are 25–31 % (SmolLM3) and 39–52 % (Granite) above it.
* **A cumulative rule would not have closed the gap.** Applying the 15 % threshold against the dense model instead
  of the parent would have stopped the runs after 7–20 steps (110–378 M parameters removed). There the estimated
  loss in the worst category was +3.0 to +17.7 points.

### 5.3 C6: does KL pick a better fallback than gradient rank 2?

**Data:** at each of 21 rank-1 rejections in arm (c), the hold-out worst-category net flip of KL's pick was compared
with gradient rank 2's, pooled over models and seeds. Source ⟨study⟩ `c6`; `tables/c6.md`.

* **KL vs rank 2:** KL better in 11, worse in 8, tied in 2. Two-sided sign test p = 0.648. Excluding the pilot: 7, 8
  and 2, p = 1.00.
* **Secondary:** TV vs KL: 5, 4 and 12 ties (TV and KL picked the same candidate in those 12). S1c vs KL: 5, 8 and 8.
* **Not established.** The pilot's 4 of 4 did not replicate.
* **Caveat on the unit.** The pre-registered unit is the rejection, but rejections cluster within runs. KL was better
  in 4 of 4 in the pilot run, in 1 of 5 in SmolLM3 seeds 1–2, and in 6 of 10 untied rejections in Granite ⟨report⟩.
  The sign test treats them as independent.

* **Arm (c)** used up to 99,959 of its 100,000-question cap ⟨study⟩.

## 6. EXPLORATORY: loss curves and the certification frontier

These analyses were not pre-registered. They use only the already-scored confirmation outcomes, and they make no
claim.

**Loss curves (Figures 1–2)** ⟨curves⟩. The per-category paired loss with one-sided 95 % bounds at every frozen
checkpoint:
* **Arm (a)** climbs steadily; Social Sciences crosses 3 points first in most seeds.
* **Arms (b) and (c)** stay within about −1 to +4 points.
* **Confirmation stops** where the upper bound of some category crosses 3 points: Social Sciences in every SmolLM3
  run; Other in every Granite run, with STEM and Social Sciences in some.

**Certification frontier (Figure 3, `tables/frontier.md`)** ⟨frontier⟩. The pre-registered procedure at limits of
2–5 points, at 500, 1,000 and 2,000 items per category (50 stratified subsamples each), and at the full set. Medians,
in % of parameters, at full n:

| limit | SmolLM3, any arm | Granite, (a) and (b) | Granite, (c) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 points | 0.61 | 1.24 | 1.24 |
| 3 points | 1.84 | 1.86 | 2.48 |
| 5 points | 3.07 | 2.48 | 4.35 |

Certified compression grows with both the limit and n.
* At 500 items per category the median is 0 at 2–3 points, and 0.61 % (SmolLM3) or 1.24 % (Granite) at 5 points.
* At a 3-point limit, going from 1,000 items to the full set triples SmolLM3's median (0.61 → 1.84 %).

**The 3-point column reproduces every recorded claim, and the S3 and S4 values;** the analysis checks this before
writing ⟨frontier⟩.

**A tighter per-category bound** (method development on seen data; it selects a method and is not a claim). The plan
`docs/bound_development_plan.md` was committed before any candidate touched these data.
* **The test.** An exact unconditional score test for the paired difference, computed on the (lost, agree, gained)
  trinomial. The statistic is Nam's (1997) and Tango's (1998); exact unconditional versions are Sidik's (2003), among
  others. It is standard in non-inferiority trials, and we do not claim it. "Exact" is up to the resolution of the
  nuisance-parameter grid (400 points) over which the supremum is taken.
* **Simulation:** it holds coverage ≥ 0.95 over the plan's grid (worst 0.9502). At a 1-point true loss and 6–9 %
  disagreement, it needs 0.49–0.51× the items of the Clopper–Pearson union bound. A betting confidence sequence needs
  more items than the union bound (1.05–1.84×) ⟨bdsim⟩.
* **These data:** it certifies no additional checkpoint on arm (a)'s trajectories. The checkpoints just past the
  claims either miss narrowly or have estimates above 3 points ⟨bdreal⟩.
* It adds one checkpoint in Granite's arm (c), seeds 0 and 1 ⟨bdreal⟩.

## 7. Limitations

* **Two 2–3 B base models, three seeds, MMLU-format items only, 0-shot.** MMLU is public and may be in pretraining
  data; MMLU-CF was built to be contamination-free.
* **One operator and one ranker.** Every run removes MLP channels chosen by gradient top-1 and leaves the remaining
  weights unchanged. Operators that update the remaining weights after each removal (as SparseGPT does; Frantar &
  Alistarh, 2023) might certify more. One exploratory check on the development model (Qwen3-1.7B, 12,000 previously
  seen items) did not show it. With and without refitting each pruned layer's output weights by ridge least squares,
  at identical masks, both trajectories stayed within 2 points for the same 13 steps (5.9 % of parameters), and none
  of the 52 paired step-by-category comparisons over those steps differed ⟨comp⟩. Over the same steps the refit cut
  the layer's reconstruction error by about 30 % (median; a third over all 40 steps) and, from step 9 on, lowered
  perplexity. That is one refit rule on one model, with masks chosen for the uncompensated model.
* **Arm (a) never rejected.** Its 15 % threshold is relative to the parent checkpoint. The comparison with the
  capability gate is therefore a comparison with an unscreened trajectory, and it says nothing about a stricter
  perplexity rule. One stricter rule is examined post hoc in §5.2.
* **About 2 % compression.** At these sizes, certified training-free compression is small. Large compression in the
  literature uses retraining at the scale of billions of tokens (Minitron). We certify small steps; we do not compete
  with it.
* **The guarantee is per category, at the population each sealed set samples,** per run at α = 0.05. Runs and arms
  are not corrected against each other.
* **The shared-trajectory design** fixes the sign of the primary comparisons (§4.1) and made C4's first criterion
  unattainable (§5.2).
* **Secondary endpoints S1–S4 were added after the pilot,** and the pilot's values are post hoc.
* **Deviations (⟨dev⟩):** GPU memory contention inflated model 1's confirmation wall time and GPU-second costs. Brief
  CPU-only test runs overlapped two screening runs. The batches ran at different commits with identical screening
  code.
* **C6's unit.** Rejections cluster within runs (§5.3), and 21 rejections are few.
* **The C1 mechanism and the frontier are exploratory** and rest on 8 category points and 6 model–seed pairs.

## 8. Related work

From `docs/paper/related_work.md`, which records what was read verbatim and what rests on summaries.

* **Compression metrics.** Dutta et al. (2024) propose flips and KL divergence as distance metrics for compressed
  LLMs, and show they are well correlated. Felicio (2026) shows that total variation converts to decision flips more
  stably than KL across reports. Our net flip loss and KL signals are these metrics, applied per MMLU category; our
  gate turns flip counts into per-category tests.
* **Multistep guarantees.** Zhang (2026) shows, for group-robust LLM pruning, that compression scores can fail to
  order endpoints for the worst group, and that a terminal guarantee needs fresh control. Our pool-versus-confirmation
  contrast is an instance of that point. We differ in the endpoint (per-category accuracy, paired flips) and the
  guarantee (a finite-sample test of the frozen checkpoints on sealed items).
* **Risk control by multiple testing.**
  * Learn then Test (Angelopoulos et al., 2021) gives fixed-sequence testing for choosing a parameter.
  * Pareto Testing (Laufer-Goldshtein et al., 2022) controls several risks with a "some risk exceeds its limit" null
    and max-p combination. That is our per-category IUT with the categories as risks.
  * Alvarez (2024) uses LTT for a magnitude-pruning ratio in vision, with a marginal guarantee.
  * Adaptive LTT (Zecchin et al., 2025) uses e-processes to save evaluations.

  Our confirmation rule is an application of this machinery, not a new test.
* **Non-inferiority tests for paired proportions.** Our per-category decision is a non-inferiority test on paired
  binary outcomes. Score tests for it are due to Nam (1997) and Tango (1998), and exact unconditional versions to
  Sidik (2003). The intersection-union principle is Berger's (1982; Berger & Hsu, 1996).
* **Risk control for efficient inference.** Jazbec et al. (2024) tune early-exit thresholds with distribution-free
  risk control, so that early exits stay within a chosen risk relative to the full model. We apply the same family of
  guarantees to pruning, per category.
* **Statistics of LLM evaluation.** Miller (2024) recommends paired analyses when two models answer the same
  questions. Our lost and gained counts are that paired design.
* **Pruning with retraining.** Minitron (Muralidharan et al., 2024; Sreenivas et al., 2024) reaches large compression
  with distillation on tens to hundreds of billions of tokens. It sets the context, not a comparison.
* **The base loop.** Kodathala & Vunnam (2026) prune with an LLM agent under a perplexity rollback (τ = 0.15),
  without statistical tests. Our arm (a) applies their threshold to our own operator and ranker. It is not a
  replication of their system, which sets per-layer sparsity with an LLM agent and prunes within a layer by Wanda's
  score, and our results for arm (a) do not transfer to it.

## 9. Conclusion

**The certified result.** Screen-then-confirm turns a screened pruning trajectory into a per-category, finite-sample
claim. On two 2–3 B models at a 3-point limit with about 3,000 sealed items per category, it certifies 1.2–3.1 % of
parameters.

**What the screens differ in.** Under a shared trajectory, the screens differ less in what they certify than in how
far they overshoot and what they cost:
* the perplexity rollback never rejected a step and overshot by about 16–17 % of parameters, yet after confirmation
  it certified as much as the capability gate in every pair, at the lowest evaluation cost;
* the capability gate overshot by at most 1.8 %, and its questions bought no additional certified compression;
* the KL fallback added a step only when the gate, not confirmation, was the binding constraint (two pairs), at the
  highest cost.

**The practical reading.** In this setting the confirmation step does the certifying, and the cheapest screen was
enough. The capability gate alone is not a certificate: its final checkpoint was not certified in four of six pairs.

**The hypotheses.** None of the three pre-registered hypotheses we tested (C1, C4, C6) is supported, and one (C4)
could not have been under this design.

**What would raise the certifiable compression** (EXPLORATORY): larger confirmation sets, a looser limit, or a tighter
per-category bound (§6). A bound that halves the items needed in simulation did not extend any perplexity-arm claim on
these trajectories.
